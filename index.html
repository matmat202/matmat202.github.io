Simulations

Physics you can touch.

Three systems, solved live in your browser at 60 frames a second — plain JavaScript on typed arrays, no libraries, no server. Drag on the stage to interact.

Incompressible fluid

Navier–Stokes on a grid, the “stable fluids” method: each step carries velocity and dye backwards along the flow, then solves a Poisson equation for pressure so the flow stays divergence-free. Vorticity confinement puts back the small eddies the grid would smear out.

∂u/∂t = −(u·∇)u − ∇p + f,   ∇·u = 0

Drag to push the fluid and drop dye. Leave it alone and a phantom spoon keeps stirring.

N-body gravity

Every body pulls on every other. The integrator is a kick–drift–kick leapfrog, which is symplectic: the energy readout wobbles but does not creep, so orbits do not spiral in or out over time the way they do with a naive method. Bodies that touch merge, conserving momentum.

ai = Σj Gmj(rj − ri) / |rj − ri|3

Drag back and let go to launch a planet, slingshot-style. The dotted line is its predicted path, computed by running the whole system forward.

Reaction–diffusion

Gray–Scott: two chemicals diffuse at different rates while one feeds on the other. That imbalance alone is enough to make spots, stripes and coral — the mechanism Turing proposed for animal markings.

∂v/∂t = Dv∇2v + uv2 − (F+k)v

Drag to seed it. Each preset is a different point (F, k) on Pearson’s map.
